Glass is made mostly from ordinary sand, which is largely silica. Heated to around 1,700 degrees Celsius, silica melts into a thick liquid, and when it cools quickly its atoms freeze in a disordered arrangement instead of forming crystals. Glassmakers add soda ash to lower the melting point and limestone to make the result durable. Around two thousand years ago, craftsmen in the eastern Mediterranean discovered that molten glass could be blown into shapes through a hollow pipe, which made glass vessels cheap enough for everyday use. Modern flat window glass is produced by floating molten glass on a bath of liquid tin, giving it a perfectly smooth surface.
