A honeybee colony behaves less like a crowd of individuals and more like a single organism. A healthy hive in summer may contain tens of thousands of workers, a few hundred males called drones, and one queen whose main job is laying eggs. Workers change roles as they age: young bees clean cells and feed larvae, middle-aged bees build comb and guard the entrance, and the oldest bees fly out to collect nectar and pollen. Foragers share the location of good flowers through the "waggle dance," in which the angle of the dance indicates direction relative to the sun and its duration indicates distance. Beyond producing honey, bees pollinate a large share of the crops people eat, which is why declines in bee populations worry farmers and scientists alike.
