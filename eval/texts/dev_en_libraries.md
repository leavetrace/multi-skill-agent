Public libraries have quietly reinvented themselves many times. In the nineteenth century, many cities opened free lending libraries so that workers could educate themselves without buying expensive books. In the twentieth century, libraries added children's rooms, music collections and reading programs. Today, a typical branch lends e-books and laptops, offers free internet access, hosts language classes and helps people fill in job applications or government forms online. Some libraries even lend tools, seeds or musical instruments. Librarians argue that the core mission has never changed: giving everyone equal access to information and a quiet, welcoming place to use it. As more services move online, that mission increasingly includes teaching people how to judge whether information is reliable.
