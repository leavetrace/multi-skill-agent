Ocean currents act as a planet-wide conveyor belt for heat. Surface currents are pushed mainly by wind, while deep currents are driven by differences in temperature and salt content: cold, salty water is dense, so it sinks near the poles and slowly spreads along the ocean floor. The Gulf Stream carries warm water from the Gulf of Mexico across the Atlantic, which helps explain why winters in western Europe are milder than those at similar latitudes in Canada. A full loop of this global circulation is thought to take around a thousand years. Because melting ice adds fresh water to the North Atlantic and makes it less dense, some climate scientists are studying whether parts of this system could weaken over the coming century.
