Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity using the photovoltaic effect. Each panel is made of many cells, usually built from thin wafers of silicon. When light strikes a cell, it knocks electrons loose, and an electric field inside the cell pushes those electrons in one direction, creating a current. Typical rooftop panels today convert around twenty percent of the sunlight that reaches them into electricity. Because panels produce direct current, an inverter is needed to change it into the alternating current used by homes and the grid. The price of solar modules has fallen dramatically over the past two decades, but the biggest remaining challenge is storage: the sun does not shine at night, so batteries or other sources are needed to keep the lights on.
