The first bicycles of the early 1800s had no pedals at all. Riders sat on a wooden frame and pushed themselves along with their feet, which is why the machine was nicknamed the "running machine." Pedals attached directly to the front wheel appeared in the 1860s, and to go faster, designers made that front wheel larger and larger, producing the famous high-wheeler, or "penny-farthing." These bikes were quick but dangerous, because a sudden stop could throw the rider over the handlebars. The real breakthrough came in the 1880s with the "safety bicycle," which used two wheels of equal size and a chain to drive the rear wheel. Add the air-filled rubber tire, introduced soon after, and the basic design we ride today was essentially complete.
