Sleep is not simply a period when the brain switches off. During the night, the brain moves through several stages roughly every ninety minutes, alternating between deep, slow-wave sleep and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, when most vivid dreaming occurs. Researchers believe deep sleep plays a key role in consolidating facts and skills learned during the day, while REM sleep appears to help with emotional processing. Sleep may also have a cleaning function: studies in mice suggest that fluid flow around brain cells increases during sleep, helping to clear away waste proteins. Adults who regularly sleep less than seven hours tend to show slower reaction times and poorer concentration, even when they feel fine. Keeping a consistent schedule, limiting screens before bed and avoiding caffeine late in the day are among the simplest ways to improve sleep quality.
